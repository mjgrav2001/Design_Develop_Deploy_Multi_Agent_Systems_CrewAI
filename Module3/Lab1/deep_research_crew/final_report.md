### 1. Overview of Edgar Allan Poe's Life and Works
Edgar Allan Poe was born on January 19, 1809, in Boston, Massachusetts. His parents were both actors but died when he was very young, leaving him an orphan. He was taken in by John and Frances Allan but never formally adopted, which led to a strained relationship. Poe faced significant personal struggles, including poverty, addiction, and the early death of his wife Virginia from tuberculosis, profoundly influencing his writing. His major literary contributions include works such as "The Raven," "The Tell-Tale Heart," and "The Fall of the House of Usher," which explore themes of isolation, death, and the grotesque.
